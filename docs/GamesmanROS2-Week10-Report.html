The honest summary

We over-delivered on understanding the system and under-delivered on making a new game work. The reason is the same in both cases: the system had more wrong with it than last semester's write-up suggested.

What changed the plan

Two things we did not anticipate — a missing hardware dependency that blocks all physical testing, and a structural assumption in the coordinate system that rules out exactly the kind of game Pong Hau K'i is.

Week 10 · Missed

Pong Hau K'i is not running

Where it actually stands

Pong Hau K'i is not registered in centers.py or in the game-type map. No board coordinates exist for it. It has never been attempted on hardware, because no game has — the hardware path is blocked by a missing dependency we only discovered in week 5.

What does run

Dodgem plays itself end to end. Nineteen solver-driven moves, 171 motion commands, real UWAPI values, the full coordinate pipeline, against a simulated arm.

That exercises every layer of the system except the physical one — and it is how we know the architecture works before the robot is available.

Two reasons, one avoidable

1. A blocked hardware path. Two nodes depend on a message package that is not in the repository and is not a stock ROS 2 package. Nothing reaches the robot until it is resolved. We found this in week 5 and have not yet closed it — that delay is on us.

2. Pong Hau K'i does not fit the system's shape. This one is not a scheduling problem. The board is not a grid, and every coordinate and vision component we have assumes one. Explained on the next slide.

Choosing Pong Hau K'i was reasonable — it is one of the simplest games in GamesmanUni, five cells and two pieces each. It turned out to be simple as a game and awkward as a board.

Week 10 · Root cause

The board is a graph, not a grid

Three components assume a grid

  • centers.py — one dim per game, scaling is 150 / dim
  • CoordConverter — square board, flips rows against dim
  • vision_node — divides the image into rows × cols cells

Not a special case

The same assumption already breaks two games we do have: 1dchess is 8×1, and dinododgem has an irregular exit region. Pong Hau K'i is the third instance, not an exception.

Why this is the useful finding

The week 15 promise is "support all games". That was a goal without a plan. It now has one: replace the grid model with an explicit cell list and adjacency. Missing week 10 is what turned week 15 from aspiration into a specification.

Beyond the deliverables

What we built that nobody asked for

Unblocked the team

Simulated hardware

One robot, three people, and the robot is not ready. We built a stand-in for the arm and gripper, so the entire stack runs with nothing plugged in.

All three workstreams proceed in parallel instead of queueing for hardware — and the architecture was proven correct before the robot was available.

Fixed a whole class of bug

Failures now travel

In the old system a failed motion returned false and was discarded — the robot silently skipped moves. Every long operation is now an action that reports success or failure to its caller.

This is what makes the hybrid planner on the next slide possible: you cannot fall back from a failure you never hear about.

Pulled forward from week 15

Vision, early

The proposal listed computer vision as future work for later semesters. It is implemented now: AR tags on pieces, located through a four-marker homography, with no camera calibration step.

It also corrects pick positions — the robot reaches for where a piece actually is, not where it should be.

Unpromised work
07

Course corrections

Two decisions we reversed, and why

Partway through we read last semester's thesis properly. It showed that two things we were replacing had been chosen deliberately, with data behind them. We reverted both.

We planned: replace the motion planner

The previous planner fails when no straight-line path exists. We intended to swap it for one that searches joint space instead.

We learned: it was chosen for a reason

Straight-line motion is predictable above a dense board — it will not swing the arm through neighbouring pieces. The thesis reports 92% success, and the failures were gripper collisions, not planning.

We did: both

Straight-line first, joint-space only when it fails. Predictable by default, robust at the edges. We also wrote a tool to measure how often the fallback is actually needed, rather than arguing about it.

We planned: replace AR tags with colour detection

Colour thresholding needs no markers on the pieces and no training data. It seemed simpler.

We learned: they had already rejected it

Colour fails when two pieces share a colour, and costs processing time on a Raspberry Pi. The thesis evaluated it explicitly and chose AR tags instead — because the tag identifies the specific piece, not its appearance.

We did: AR tags, better plumbed

Kept their approach, dropped the fragile part — the camera calibration and transform chain — and replaced it with a homography from four board-corner markers. Same robustness, far fewer moving parts.

Both reversals cost time. Both were cheaper than discovering the same things on hardware in week 14 — and the generalisation argument for AR tags is what makes the week 15 deliverable plausible at all.

Revised week 15 target

Supporting every game is not reachable in five weeks with hardware still unvalidated. A target we can defend:

  • Replace the grid model with explicit cell lists and adjacency
  • Pong Hau K'i and Dodgem both running on the physical robot
  • All 9 existing games passing the coordinate check

Why that is the right scope

Removing the grid assumption is the single change that unlocks the most games, and it is the one we can only make now that we know it is the obstacle. Two games running on real hardware proves the pipeline end to end; nine games passing the check proves the data layer.

Risks

What stands between here and week 15

Highest risk

Missing message package

Two nodes import a package that is not in the repository and is not a stock ROS 2 package. Until it is resolved, nothing runs on the robot and the week 15 hardware goal is unreachable.

Mitigation: vendor the vendor's package. Their driver publishes those exact types, and ROS 2 matches messages by package name, so defining our own would not interoperate.

Hardware never validated

Everything is proven in simulation. Physical measurements — where the board sits, whether every square is reachable — are still unconfirmed. A measurement tool is written and ready to run.

Five games still mis-scaled

Three use a different coordinate convention, one has no dimension at all, one is both. Each is quarantined behind a failing test so none can be forgotten.

Failed moves corrupt game state

Known, reproducible, and small. The fix is not the hard part — deciding what should happen on failure is. Assigned.

Single point of knowledge

Most architectural context sits with one person. The onboarding deck and the three-track split exist to spread it; the test suite exists so mistakes surface without needing that context.

We promised to understand the system and then extend it.
